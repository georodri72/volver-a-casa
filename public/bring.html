What to bring

Leaving the U.S. (carry-on)

  • Liquids, aerosols, gels, creams and pastes: containers of 3.4 oz (100 ml) or less in one quart-sized bag. Larger items go in checked baggage. Medications and infant/child nourishment are exempt. Source: TSA – Liquids, Aerosols, and Gels Rule

Arriving in El Salvador

  • It is illegal to bring guns, ammunition, or spent shells/casings into El Salvador. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • Items containing THC, including most CBD products, are illegal in El Salvador. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • Carry prescription medication in its original packaging with your doctor's prescription, and check that it's legal in El Salvador. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • Groups bringing donated supplies or medicine should clear them first with the Superintendencia de Regulación Sanitaria (consultas@srs.gob.sv). Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador

Coming back to the U.S. (food from home)

  • Bakery items and certain cheeses are allowed. Many prepared foods are admissible, but almost anything containing meat is not. Source: CBP – Prohibited and Restricted Items
  • As a general rule, condiments, vinegars, oils, packaged spices, honey, coffee and tea are admissible. It's best to avoid bringing rice. Source: CBP – Prohibited and Restricted Items
  • Every plant or plant product (including straw handicrafts) must be declared to CBP, and there are civil penalties for failing to declare agricultural items. Fresh fruit and vegetables depend on where they came from and where you're going. Source: CBP – Prohibited and Restricted Items

Money

The U.S. dollar is the official currency. Some places don't take cards or charge a fee, so bring cash. ATMs are often out of order and can charge high fees. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
