Entry rules

Passport, DUI, minors and cash, with official sources.

If you are Salvadoran

  • Salvadorans prove their nationality on entry with a passport or DUI. Source: Reglamento de la Ley Especial de Migración y Extranjería (Art. 21)
  • Children of a Salvadoran mother or father who hold a foreign passport can enter as Salvadorans by presenting a birth certificate proving the parent's nationality and the relationship. Source: Reglamento de la Ley Especial de Migración y Extranjería (Art. 21)
  • Per the U.S. Embassy, if your U.S. passport shows you were born in El Salvador, neither the tourist card nor proof of funds is required. Source: U.S. Embassy San Salvador – Travel Requirements
  • To leave for Guatemala, Honduras or Nicaragua (CA-4) a DUI or passport is enough; for any other country you need a passport valid for at least 6 months. Source: Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería – Preguntas

U.S. citizens (without Salvadoran nationality)

  • No visa is required for stays of 90 days or less. Your passport must be valid at the time of entry. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • CA-4 agreement: after legally entering El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras or Nicaragua you can travel among them for up to 90 days total. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • $12 tourist card: sources disagree. The U.S. Embassy page still lists it (checked Oct 18, 2026), but Diario El Salvador reported on May 1, 2025 that the Legislative Assembly eliminated the $12 entry card fee (Tarjeta Migratoria de Ingreso). Confirm with immigration before you travel. Source: U.S. Embassy San Salvador – Travel Requirements, Diario El Salvador – eliminación de la Tarjeta Migratoria de Ingreso

Cash

The State Department lists a $10,000 (USD) maximum on entry and exit; the Embassy page says $15,000 (USD). Both pages checked Oct 18, 2026. If you carry a lot of cash, confirm the current limit. The U.S. dollar is El Salvador's official currency. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador, U.S. Embassy San Salvador – Travel Requirements

Traveling with children

  • Bring a copy of each child's birth certificate or other proof of your legal relationship. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • A U.S. minor in El Salvador for more than one year is considered a resident; to leave without a parent they need consent notarized by a Salvadoran notary (U.S. Embassy notarization is not accepted). Authorities have sometimes blocked minors traveling without a parent or guardian from leaving. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador

Health

Yellow fever vaccination is required if arriving from certain countries in South America or Africa. See CDC recommendations. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador, CDC – Travelers' Health: El Salvador
