Safety & tips

Safety, driving, health and emergencies.

The U.S. State Department rates El Salvador Level 1: Exercise normal precautions (issued June 25, 2026). Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador

Know before you go

  • Zero tolerance for drinking and driving: any blood alcohol level is a criminal offense, with 2 to 5 years in prison. Police run random checks. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • You can drive on a foreign license for up to 30 days. Avoid driving at night and on unpaved roads. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • The State of Exception (since March 2022) remains in effect and suspends some constitutional rights; several U.S. citizens have been detained under it. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • Pacific beaches have strong currents and undertows; stay out when red or black flags are up. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • Tap water is generally unsafe to drink; use major-brand bottled water with an intact seal. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • Hurricane season: June–November. Rainy season (floods, landslides): May–October. Earthquakes are frequent. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • U.S. citizens: enroll in STEP for Embassy alerts. Source: Smart Traveler Enrollment Program (STEP)

Emergencies

  • Police: 911 · Ambulance: 132 · Domestic violence hotline: 126. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
  • U.S. Embassy San Salvador: +503 2501-2999. Source: U.S. Department of State – El Salvador
